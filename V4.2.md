# WorldForge v4.2 — Living Ruins & Archaeology

WorldForge v4.2 turns the static historical landmarks introduced in v4.1 into a layered archaeological landscape. Ruins can now weather, become overgrown, be surveyed and excavated, while settlements accumulate dated historical layers that can be explored through the Worldbuilding Atelier and Universal Encyclopedia.

## Living ruin lifecycle

Every eligible historical landmark receives a deterministic lifecycle state derived from its age, current condition, biome, source and discovery status:

1. **Pristine** — the structure is still close to its modeled original form.
2. **Damaged** — visible structural loss and deterioration have begun.
3. **Ruined** — major portions have collapsed and rubble dominates the footprint.
4. **Overgrown** — vegetation and landscape reclamation obscure older remains.
5. **Excavated** — survey grids, trenches and catalogued archaeological finds expose the site again.

The same world seed and simulation state always produce the same lifecycle stage and visual result.

## Archaeological sites

Ruins and battlefields are direct archaeology candidates. Older or deteriorated Great Works, monuments and major infrastructure can also enter the archaeology layer. Each site records:

- integrity and vegetation pressure;
- modeled burial depth;
- survey confidence;
- excavation progress;
- source landmark/event references;
- deterministic artifact finds;
- inscriptions where historical significance and preservation allow them;
- a then-vs-now reconstruction record.

Site counts remain bounded per settlement so the archaeology layer stays suitable for long-running worlds.

## Artifacts and inscriptions

Sites can expose finds such as ceramics, coins, tools, weapon fragments, seals, tablets, religious tokens, trade weights, glasswork and ornaments. Finds include material, approximate date, condition, significance and catalogue state.

Historically important sites can also expose inscriptions with a modeled subject, script family, approximate date, legibility and translation state. These records are deterministic worldbuilding evidence rather than random UI decoration.

## Historical stratigraphy

Settlement-specific historical events and dated landmarks are grouped into bounded chronological layers. Each layer stores:

- period start and end;
- dominant modeled use such as urban occupation, defensive activity, maritime trade, ceremonial use or destruction horizon;
- buried/near-surface status;
- modeled depth and survival;
- links back to the source events, landmarks and archaeological sites.

This allows newer settlements to visibly sit on top of older historical occupation instead of presenting history as a flat list.

## Then vs Now reconstruction

The archaeology pixel renderer adds a dedicated historical reconstruction mode. The Worldbuilding Atelier can show a site in two deterministic views:

- **Then** — a reconstructed pristine state around the landmark's origin year.
- **Now** — the site's current lifecycle state in the simulated world.

A five-stage lifecycle strip makes the visual progression from intact structure to excavation directly inspectable.

## Pixel Civilization integration

`archaeology-pixel-renderer.js` layers v4.2 archaeology visuals on top of the v4.1.1 polished renderer without replacing the existing architectural system. Existing culture-specific building variants, district layouts and historical landmarks remain intact.

Settlement SVG output now exposes:

- `data-worldforge-archaeology="4.2.0"`
- `data-archaeology-overlay="v4.2"`
- `data-archaeology-site`
- `data-site-type`
- `data-ruin-stage`
- `data-reconstruction-mode`

## Worldbuilding Atelier

The Atelier gains a new Archaeology & Historical Layers section containing:

- archaeology KPIs;
- site selector;
- then-vs-now previews;
- five-stage ruin lifecycle strip;
- site survey record;
- artifacts and inscriptions;
- settlement stratigraphy;
- archaeology ledger export.

## Universal Encyclopedia

v4.2 adds five searchable knowledge types:

- `archaeological_site`
- `artifact_find`
- `inscription`
- `historical_layer`
- `historical_reconstruction`

These records cross-link to settlements, historical landmarks and source events where available.

## Compatibility and persistence

v4.2 is additive. It does not replace the v4.1 worldbuilding schema or v4.1.1 pixel-art polish. Archaeology state lives under `world.archaeology`, has its own schema/version marker and can be reconstructed deterministically from the existing worldbuilding and historical state.

The existing v4.0/v4.1 regression chains remain in the v4.2 CI gate. Dedicated v4.2 tests cover lifecycle states, archaeological sites, artifacts, inscriptions, historical stratigraphy, encyclopedia indexing, pixel overlays, then-vs-now reconstruction, deterministic fingerprints and save/export round trips.
