# WorldForge v2.6 — Health, Disease, Fire & Emergency Response

v2.6 turns the risk values created by v2.5 Municipal Infrastructure & Public Services into historical consequences. Weak water and sanitation, overcrowding, poor healthcare, dense housing, slums and weak fire protection can now create outbreaks, quarantines, urban fires, casualties, property loss and emergency spending.

## Aggregate health simulation

WorldForge does not simulate every person as an individual disease agent. Each settlement keeps bounded aggregate health state:

- susceptible population;
- infected population;
- recovered population;
- annual new cases;
- annual disease deaths;
- outbreak severity;
- quarantine status.

This keeps the system compatible with very long Endless Simulation runs while still making population and infrastructure choices matter.

## Disease families

The initial deterministic pathogen catalog contains:

- River Fever;
- Grey Pox;
- Dockside Flux;
- Red Cough;
- Marsh Ague.

Pathogens differ in transmission and fatality characteristics. Outbreak pressure is generated from real simulation inputs rather than arbitrary random events.

```text
Water / sanitation failure
 + overcrowding
 + municipal health risk
 + settlement connectivity
 → disease hazard
 → outbreak / transmission
 → cases / recovery / deaths
 → quarantine / emergency response
 → population / prosperity / migration effects
```

Healthcare coverage, emergency response capacity, education and quarantine reduce effective transmission or mortality pressure.

## Urban fire simulation

Fire risk consumes the district and municipal conditions created in v2.4 and v2.5:

- municipal fire risk;
- housing density;
- slum share;
- fire-protection coverage;
- water coverage;
- road access;
- emergency response capacity.

High-risk settlements can suffer an Urban Fire with persistent annual consequences:

- casualties;
- assessed property loss;
- lower property index;
- weaker development;
- increased slum pressure;
- higher municipal maintenance backlog;
- migration and prosperity pressure.

Major fires become `urban_fire` History events and can therefore propagate into WorldForge's knowledge, wiki and story systems.

## Emergency response and finance

A settlement receives a response-capacity and preparedness score from existing simulation state. Important inputs include:

- healthcare coverage;
- fire-service coverage;
- road access;
- utility reliability;
- municipal public-works capacity;
- household education.

Emergency response has a real fiscal cost. Response funding first draws from municipal reserves. If local reserves are insufficient, a bounded amount can be taken from the owning realm's treasury. Same-year simulation is idempotent, preventing duplicated emergency spending, duplicated casualties or duplicated History events.

## Quarantine

Sufficiently severe outbreaks can place a settlement under quarantine. Quarantine reduces transmission but creates additional migration and economic pressure. The quarantine state is stored explicitly and exposed through the Emergency Command Center and Universal Encyclopedia.

## District alerts

The v2.5 district service profiles are reused rather than duplicated. Up to eight highest-risk district alerts per settlement are retained in the emergency profile.

Districts are classified as:

- normal;
- watch;
- critical.

The classification uses public-health and fire-risk values already produced by the municipal layer.

## History integration

Severe incidents can become canonical History events:

- `epidemic` for significant disease outbreaks;
- `urban_fire` for major city fires.

These events use stable incident IDs so repeated bootstrap or UI calls cannot create duplicate history entries.

## Universal Encyclopedia

v2.6 adds three article types:

- `Emergency Status`;
- `Disease Outbreak`;
- `Urban Fire`.

Articles cross-link settlements with their municipal services and infrastructure state. Outbreak and fire articles preserve the incident year, severity, casualties, quarantine state and losses.

## Emergency Command Center

The new workspace exposes:

- active outbreaks;
- active fires;
- annual cases;
- annual deaths;
- fire property loss;
- emergency spending;
- quarantined settlements;
- mean preparedness;
- settlement disease/fire hazards;
- active infected population;
- district alerts;
- recent incident history;
- standalone Emergency Ledger JSON export.

## Optimization

v2.6 continues the bounded-state architecture introduced in v2.3–v2.5.

### Runtime indexes

WeakMap-backed indexes provide O(1)-style access to:

- municipal profiles;
- housing profiles;
- household profiles;
- workforce markets;
- cities;
- realm treasury accounts;
- emergency profiles.

These caches are runtime-only and are never serialized into the world save.

### Bounded persistent state

WorldForge stores one emergency profile per settlement, compact aggregate disease compartments and a bounded incident ledger. It does not store individual patients, firefighters, hospital beds, fire engines, individual burning buildings or per-person infection histories.

### Rendering

The Emergency Command Center uses revision-driven rendering, `requestAnimationFrame` coalescing and hidden-tab suspension, matching the optimization pattern used by v2.4 and v2.5.

## Determinism and regression coverage

The v2.6 gate re-runs the v2.5 municipal regression before running the new emergency regression. It verifies:

- emergency profile coverage for every settlement;
- bounded disease and fire hazards;
- bounded outbreak and fire severity;
- Universal Encyclopedia integration;
- WeakMap runtime-cache reuse;
- same-year casualty idempotency;
- same-year emergency-finance idempotency;
- deterministic `4 years == 2 + 2 years` caller chunking;
- preservation of municipal, housing, household and workforce determinism;
- save/export round-trip stability;
- high-risk settlements can produce outbreaks and fires;
- major fires enter canonical History.

## Causal chain

```text
Housing + public infrastructure
 → disease / fire hazard
 → incident
 → emergency response
 → casualties / property loss / fiscal cost
 → population / prosperity / housing / migration
 → future municipal tax base and service demand
```
