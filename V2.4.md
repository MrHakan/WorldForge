# WorldForge v2.4 — Housing, Land Ownership & Urban Development

v2.4 extends the v2.3 household economy into a physical property system. Population growth, wages, poverty and migration now meet a finite dwelling stock, land ownership, rent, construction capacity and district-level urban pressure.

## Physical housing stock

Every settlement tracks:

- dwelling units, occupied homes and vacancies;
- explicit housing shortages;
- rent and property-price indices;
- rent burden;
- overcrowding;
- homeless households;
- owner-occupancy, rental and informal tenure;
- annual construction demand, starts, completions and demolitions.

Housing is not generated as an isolated percentage. Demand starts from the actual v2.3 household count. Construction capacity is linked to the local Construction workforce and urban scale. New dwellings arrive through an annual construction pipeline while a small part of the old stock is removed through decay/demolition.

## Land ownership

Land is divided into five contextual ownership classes:

1. Crown / State
2. Aristocratic Estates
3. Religious Holdings
4. Merchant Property
5. Smallholders & Commons

Shares are derived from settlement status and the v2.3 social-class structure. Capitals tend to hold more state land, aristocratic households expand estate ownership, clergy strengthens religious holdings, merchants accumulate commercial property and non-elite households support the smallholder/common pool.

## Housing tenure

Households are distributed across:

- owner occupied;
- private rent;
- estate tenancy;
- religious / charitable tenancy;
- informal housing.

Poverty and housing shortages reduce ownership and can expand informal housing. Land ownership therefore influences the way households experience the property market rather than acting as a decorative statistic.

## District development

Existing City Engine districts receive housing metadata without creating an unbounded number of new district entities:

- housing units;
- density;
- rent index;
- overcrowding;
- building condition;
- informal units;
- land value.

Low-income districts such as Slums, Low Quarter and Outskirts absorb more crowding and informal construction, while Wealthy Quarter, Government and Old Town districts generally command higher land values and rents.

This keeps the city simulation bounded while still making urban form react to the property economy.

## Causal feedback

```text
Population / migration
 → household demand
 → dwelling shortage or vacancy
 → rent / property values
 → rent burden
 → poverty / overcrowding / homelessness
 → migration pressure + city crime pressure
 → construction demand
 → construction workforce capacity
 → new dwellings
 → shortage relief
```

Housing cost feeds back into the v2.3 household profile by reducing disposable income and increasing poverty when rent burden or crowding becomes excessive. Housing shortages also add outward migration pressure, while unusually high vacancy can reduce it.

## Property Register UI

The new workspace exposes:

- world dwelling, vacancy and shortage totals;
- homeless-household counts;
- mean rent, property, overcrowding and ownership indicators;
- settlement housing-market cards;
- district density, condition, crowding and land value;
- weighted land-ownership shares;
- construction demand / starts / completions;
- standalone Property Ledger JSON export.

Housing Market, Land Ownership and Urban Development are first-class Universal Encyclopedia article types.

## Optimization pass

v2.4 deliberately includes a performance pass because WorldForge now has many annual simulation layers.

### Indexed annual lookups

The household engine no longer repeatedly scans full arrays for every settlement when it needs:

- resource markets;
- active facilities;
- facility type counts;
- city records;
- active sieges;
- household profiles.

These are assembled into WeakMap-backed runtime indexes. They are rebuilt when the simulation advances but are not serialized into saves.

The housing engine uses the same indexed approach for city, household and housing profile lookups.

### Save-state discipline

Runtime caches live outside the world JSON through `WeakMap`, so optimization metadata does not increase save/export size. Housing keeps one aggregate profile per settlement plus bounded district summaries rather than creating every physical household or individual dwelling as a persistent entity.

### UI rendering

The Household Register and Property Register use revision counters instead of recomputing a full deterministic fingerprint on every polling pass. DOM updates are coalesced through `requestAnimationFrame`, polling is reduced, and periodic rendering is suspended while the browser tab is hidden.

### Regression coverage

The v2.4 gate re-runs the full v2.3 household regression after the optimization changes, then verifies:

- housing profile coverage;
- land and tenure accounting;
- physical dwelling accounting;
- rent and property indices;
- construction pipeline state;
- district housing integration;
- Universal Encyclopedia integration;
- housing → household feedback;
- property tax-base calculation;
- runtime cache reuse across repeated reads;
- deterministic `4 years == 2 + 2 years` simulation;
- workforce and household determinism after housing feedback;
- save/export round-trip stability.
