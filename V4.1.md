# WorldForge v4.1 — Historical Landscape & Architecture

WorldForge v4.1 deepens the v4.0 Pixel Civilization layer by making architecture culturally distinct and allowing history to remain visible in the physical landscape.

## Culture-specific architecture

v4.0 already used culture and biome to influence palette and settlement identity. v4.1 adds actual architectural form families so different cultures no longer differ only through color.

Eight architecture families are defined:

- **Imperial Masonry** — courtyard houses, formal stone civic buildings and monumental temples.
- **Northern Timber** — longhouses, steep roofs, timber halls and beacon towers.
- **Maritime Stucco** — waterfront houses, quayside warehouses, coastal temples and beacons.
- **Highland Stone** — stone houses, tower houses, hill towers and mountain gates.
- **Frontier Stockade** — timber dwellings, trading posts, palisades and stockade gates.
- **Mercantile Urban** — row houses, guild houses, covered markets, exchanges and counting warehouses.
- **Riverine Timber** — stilt houses, river houses, quays, waterside warehouses and bridges.
- **Steppe Court** — yurt clusters, caravan houses, khan halls, shrines and earthworks.

The base simulation archetype remains stable. A `house` is still a residential house to the simulation, but the renderer resolves it to an appropriate cultural visual variant. This keeps simulation data compact and avoids duplicating economic entities for every art style.

## Historical landscape

Each settlement can now expose a bounded list of visible landmarks derived from existing simulation state. The worldbuilding layer does not invent a competing history database.

Sources include:

- active Adventure sites and ruins;
- Great Works from the Institution layer;
- major settlement-bound wars, battles, conquests, sieges, collapses, fires, famines and disease/disaster events;
- settlement geography and infrastructure state.

Supported visual landmark classes are:

- ruin;
- battlefield;
- Great Work;
- lighthouse;
- bridge;
- canal;
- fortified port;
- monument.

Each landmark records its source, origin year, age, condition, importance and deterministic visual position. Profiles retain at most 10 landmarks per settlement.

## Infrastructure landmarks

Important infrastructure can become part of the visual identity when local simulation conditions justify it:

- important maritime trade centers can gain sea beacons;
- sufficiently populated river settlements can gain bridges;
- developed river trade hubs can gain commercial canals;
- defended coastal settlements can gain fortified harbors;
- major capitals can gain civic monuments.

These are derived summaries, not individual persistent construction simulations.

## Pixel renderer

The SVG renderer now supports culture-specific structure variants and dedicated landmark glyphs. The v4.0 pixel format marker is retained for compatibility, while the renderer reports `data-worldforge-renderer="v4.1"` and a settlement architecture-family marker.

Historical ruins, battlefields, Great Works and strategic infrastructure can therefore appear directly in settlement previews.

## Worldbuilding Atelier

The Atelier adds:

- historical-landscape KPIs;
- per-settlement landmark cards;
- architecture-family metadata;
- primary material, roof type and street pattern;
- architecture signature text;
- landmark source, origin, age, importance and condition.

## Universal Encyclopedia

v4.1 adds first-class article types:

- `historical_landmark`;
- `architecture_style`.

Settlement and visual-identity articles cross-link to architecture families and visible landmarks.

## Performance and save compatibility

The v4.1 system remains aggregate and bounded:

- maximum 10 landmark summaries per settlement;
- no persistent building-per-entity model;
- Adventure sites, Great Works and relevant history events are indexed in runtime-only `WeakMap` caches;
- landmark positions are deterministic and derived;
- old v4.0 worldbuilding profiles are upgraded by rebuilding only the derived worldbuilding layer;
- the underlying History, City, Housing, Municipal, Institution, Adventure and World Order state is not rewritten.

## Causal relationships

```text
History events ───────────────┐
Adventure sites / ruins ─────┤
Institution Great Works ─────┤
Culture + biome ──────────────┤
Trade + river/coast ──────────┤
Defense + municipal services ─┤
                              ↓
                 Historical landscape profile
                              ↓
        architecture + landmarks + pixel scene
```

## Regression coverage

The v4.1 gate verifies:

- all eight architecture families;
- culture-specific structure form resolution;
- valid bounded landmark profiles;
- deterministic SVG rendering;
- v4.0 renderer-format compatibility;
- architecture and landmark Encyclopedia integration;
- Adventure-site → ruin causality;
- Institution Great Work → landmark causality;
- History battle → battlefield causality;
- runtime-index reuse;
- same-year idempotency;
- deterministic `4 years == 2 + 2` simulation chunking;
- World Order and Workforce compatibility;
- save/export round trips.
