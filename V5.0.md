# WorldForge v5.0 — Strategic AI & War Consequences

v5.0 turns v4.9 warfare into a persistent strategic campaign layer while keeping warfare, diplomacy, logistics and peace systems authoritative.

## Implemented milestones

### Strategic AI Core
Active wars generate deterministic fronts, army assignments, campaign objectives and siege-derived occupation control.

### Commanders & Leadership
Persistent commanders have skill, seniority, prestige and situational traits with bounded combat effects.

### Strategic Reserves & Reinforcements
Front collapse risk creates reinforcement requests; suitable reserve armies are ranked and committed with fulfillment tracking.

### Multi-Year Campaign Planning & Objective Reassessment
Campaigns persist momentum, posture, phase, priority and reassessment history across years. Failed offensives can fall back to hold-line while recovered campaigns can resume territorial objectives.

### Occupation Policy, Resistance, Collaboration & Liberation
Occupation policies, resistance, collaboration, administrative control, liberation risk and liberated-history persistence are simulated annually.

### Front-line Visualization & Campaign Arrows
A non-interactive SVG layer renders fronts, campaign arrows, reinforcement movement and occupation pressure without blocking army/fleet hit testing.

### Strategic Map Controls & Legend
Fronts, campaigns, reinforcements and occupations can be toggled independently; campaign cards select and highlight their campaign/front on the map.

### Commander Wounds, Battlefield Death, Succession & Historical Legacies
- Commander application is idempotent within a simulation year: repeated UI or `force=true` refreshes no longer multiply combat power, morale or prestige.
- Leadership stores base and commander-adjusted combat power/morale separately so the same commander modifier can be reapplied safely.
- Prestige advances only when `battleCount` actually increases; already processed battles cannot award prestige twice.
- Commanders receive deterministic annual battlefield-risk evaluation from new battles, casualty pressure, army readiness, relief duty and offensive exposure.
- Wounds have light/moderate/severe records and deterministic recovery years. Wounded commanders remain in command with a temporary leadership penalty.
- Battlefield death records `deathYear` and cause of death, archives the fallen commander and immediately creates a successor for an army that still exists.
- Retirement is deterministic and age/service based; retired commanders are archived without allowing the same identity to be regenerated.
- Army succession indices generate unique commander IDs and preserve `successorOf` lineage.
- Historical legacy score blends prestige, skill, battles and service length. Legacies are classified as remembered, renowned or legendary.
- Distinguished or fallen commanders become memorial-eligible; memorial types include battlefield monuments, commander statues, memorial halls and plaques.
- Memorials attach to the commander's army home settlement and are mirrored into the existing v4.3 heritage state when available, without replacing its registry/relic/museum state.
- Strategic UI exposes wounded, fallen and legacy KPIs, recovery year and predecessor linkage, plus a dedicated Commander Legacies section.
- Behavioral regression covers same-year idempotency, battle prestige deduplication, bounded command modifiers, succession uniqueness, deterministic fatal-risk discovery and heritage memorial bridging.

### Historical Battlefields, War Scars, Archaeology & Heritage
- Completed warfare battle records persist as long-lived historical war scars instead of disappearing with the current-year battle list.
- Each scar derives deterministic significance from casualties, battle scale, battle balance and relief/siege context.
- Persistent scar categories include battlefields, siegeworks, war scars and minor skirmish sites.
- Scars retain battle identity, year, settlement, casualty totals, outcome, attacker/defender references, physical condition and memorial potential.
- War scars age across simulation years. Repeated same-year application is idempotent and cannot duplicate the same battlefield.
- Significant scars become World Building landmarks at their affected settlement and can remain visible as battlefield or ruin landmarks.
- After sufficient age and significance, scars become archaeology-eligible battlefield records with integrity, lifecycle stage and weapon/tool artifact bias.
- Older, important battlefields become heritage-eligible records with protected or national-heritage classification and commemoration metadata.
- Archaeology and Heritage bridges are additive: existing v4.2/v4.3 state remains authoritative and is not replaced.
- Warfare summary and settlement military views expose historical battlefield and local war-scar counts.
- A dedicated Battlefields & War Scars panel shows significance, casualties, physical condition, archaeology/heritage eligibility and historical outcome.
- Behavioral regression verifies scar persistence, same-year idempotency, aging, World Building landmark creation, Archaeology bridge creation, Heritage bridge creation and local city exposure.

### Battlefield Excavations, War Relics & Provenance
- Archaeology-eligible battlefields now generate deterministic excavation contexts and persistent relic records.
- Relic categories include weapon fragments, personal effects, recovered standard fragments, officer insignia and siege-equipment fragments.
- Every relic stores provenance back to its battlefield scar, battle ID, origin year and settlement.
- High-significance or commander-associated objects can become Heritage records; lower-tier finds can remain archaeology-only or museum candidates.
- Relics can link to the commander associated with the participating army, preserving commander ID/name for future biographies and museum display.
- High-casualty, high-significance battlefield contexts are marked protected to support future burial-site, memorial and preservation rules.
- Excavation and relic projections are additive to existing Archaeology and Heritage state rather than replacing v4.2/v4.3 registries.
- Repeated application with unchanged state is idempotent: existing relic identities and discovery records are not duplicated.
- A dedicated Battlefield Excavations & War Relics panel exposes provenance, rarity, condition, commander links and museum/heritage status.

## Architecture

The strategic modules wrap `WorldForgeWarfareSupply`; they do not create a second combat engine. v4.9 remains authoritative for armies, supply, sieges, naval warfare, diplomacy, post-war recovery and peace terms. v5.0 derives strategic planning, leadership consequences, historical war memory and map visualization from those systems.

Long-lived battlefield history and relic generation are loaded by the central World Building loader rather than depending on a UI being opened. Warfare owns battle identity and historical provenance; World Building, Archaeology and Heritage receive additive projections so their existing registries and simulation rules remain intact.
