# WorldForge v5.0 — Strategic AI & War Consequences

v5.0 begins turning the v4.9 warfare simulation into a strategic campaign layer. The first milestone adds fronts, campaign objectives, army assignments and occupation state without replacing the existing warfare, diplomacy, logistics or peace systems.

## Implemented — Strategic AI Core

- Active wars generate one to three deterministic fronts from the nearest opposing settlement pairs.
- Fronts track attacker/defender anchor settlements, assigned field armies, combat power balance and contested/advantage state.
- Armies receive persistent `frontId` and `strategicRole` metadata (`offensive`, `defensive`, `reserve`).
- Each active war generates a campaign plan derived from its existing v4.9 war goal.
- Campaign objective types include capture-capital, seize-border, break-army, liberate-settlement and hold-line.
- Conquest goals prioritize the defender's largest settlement; defensive goals anchor on the threatened friendly front.
- Campaigns expose target settlement, priority, front assignment and active status.
- High-pressure sieges now produce occupation-control state rather than ending at a generic siege flag.
- Occupation records track occupier, original realm, controlling army, administrative control and resistance pressure.
- Strategic summary metrics expose active fronts, campaigns, contested fronts and occupied settlements.
- Settlement military views expose associated fronts and occupation state.
- Dedicated Strategic AI & Fronts UI provides a first inspection surface for campaign planning.

## Architecture

The strategic layer wraps the existing `WorldForgeWarfareSupply` API. It does not create a second combat engine. v4.9 remains authoritative for armies, supply, sieges, naval warfare, diplomacy, post-war recovery and peace terms; v5.0 derives higher-level strategic state from those systems.
