# WorldForge v2.8 — Politics, Unrest, Legitimacy & Civil Disorder

v2.8 turns the material pressures accumulated through WorldForge's economy, households, housing, public services, war, health and food-security systems into a deterministic political-order simulation. Political instability is not generated as an isolated random event. Settlements and realms now develop grievances because of the conditions they actually experience, and authorities can answer those grievances with repression or concessions.

## Settlement political state

Every settlement receives an aggregate political profile containing:

- legitimacy;
- public order;
- grievance;
- radicalization;
- repression memory;
- concession memory;
- protest potential;
- strike potential;
- riot risk;
- coup support;
- secession support;
- a six-stage unrest classification;
- dominant grievance;
- six aggregate political blocs.

WorldForge continues to avoid person-by-person political simulation. The model keeps bounded aggregate state and representative political blocs instead.

## Six political-order stages

The settlement's public-order score produces one of six stages:

1. Stable;
2. Uneasy;
3. Tense;
4. Protest;
5. Disorder;
6. Insurrection.

The stage is descriptive but also controls event and state-response behavior.

## Causal grievances

Grievance is calculated from existing simulation systems rather than a new random crisis generator. Major inputs include:

- household poverty;
- workforce unemployment;
- housing rent burden, overcrowding and homelessness when present;
- food insecurity, malnutrition, famine stage and rationing;
- effective tax pressure;
- treasury inflation and debt stress;
- wartime conscription;
- disease pressure;
- municipal service coverage;
- inequality implied by social-class shares;
- realm stability;
- Rebel League and Political Faction influence.

This creates chains such as:

`crop failure → food shortage → rationing → bread riots → repression → radicalization → lower legitimacy`

and:

`war → surcharge + conscription → anti-war movement → concessions → lower tax rate + higher legitimacy`

## Aggregate political blocs

Each settlement tracks six political blocs:

- Commoners;
- Labor;
- Guilds;
- Merchants;
- Professionals;
- Elites.

Each bloc has a relative weight, grievance and opposition score. The bloc calculation uses v2.3 social-class shares plus pressure that is particularly important to that bloc. For example, labor reacts more strongly to unemployment and conscription, merchants to taxation and inflation, commoners to food and poverty, and elites to fiscal and regime instability.

## Factions

The existing v0.8 organization system is now materially connected to politics.

- Rebel League influence raises factional opposition and radicalization.
- Severe political grievance can increase Rebel League influence.
- Political Factions gain influence when reform and concession politics become important.
- Realm opposition influence contributes to coup, revolutionary and secession risk.

The older faction system remains the owner of organization entities; v2.8 does not duplicate faction records.

## Civil disorder events

Depending on the causal conditions of the settlement, v2.8 can record:

- protests;
- strikes;
- bread riots;
- tax revolts;
- anti-war movements;
- riots and broader civil disorder.

Events use deterministic keyed probability after material thresholds are crossed. A settlement with no meaningful grievance therefore does not receive arbitrary unrest merely because a random number was low.

## Repression and concessions

Settlements at Protest stage or worse force a state response.

### Repression

Repression can:

- restore some short-term public order;
- cost treasury resources;
- reduce legitimacy;
- increase radicalization;
- enter canonical History.

### Concessions

Concessions can:

- reduce grievance;
- reduce radicalization;
- improve legitimacy;
- cost treasury resources;
- slightly reduce the base tax rate;
- enter canonical History.

Both responses persist as decaying memory rather than disappearing immediately in the following year.

## Realm political order

Settlement profiles are population-weighted into realm political profiles:

- legitimacy;
- public order;
- grievance;
- radicalization;
- repression;
- concessions;
- opposition influence;
- coup risk;
- revolution risk;
- secession risk;
- maximum unrest stage.

At extreme conditions the simulation can record coup attempts and revolutionary crises. These events reduce realm stability but deliberately do not rewrite borders or rulers in v2.8; territorial breakup and full civil-war resolution remain future-system responsibilities.

## Cross-system feedback

Political disorder feeds back into the existing world:

- unrest raises workforce migration pressure;
- riots can worsen city crime;
- political pressure is exposed to municipal state;
- civil-disorder risk is exposed to emergency state;
- repression/concessions alter realm stability;
- state responses consume Treasury funds;
- concessions can reduce tax rates;
- Rebel League / Political Faction influence changes over time.

## Universal Encyclopedia

v2.8 adds four Encyclopedia article types:

- `political_status`;
- `realm_politics`;
- `civil_unrest`;
- `political_response`.

Political chronology links settlement and realm articles to protests, strikes, riots, bread riots, tax revolts, anti-war movements, repression, concessions, coup attempts, revolutionary crises and older faction uprisings.

## Political Stability Observatory

The new UI workspace displays:

- global legitimacy, public order and grievance KPIs;
- protest/disorder/insurrection counts;
- coup-attempt and revolutionary-crisis counts;
- realm political cards with coup/revolution/secession risks;
- settlement legitimacy, public order, grievance and radicalization;
- protest and riot risk;
- dominant grievance;
- recent civil disorder and state responses;
- political-bloc opposition;
- Political Ledger JSON export.

The UI remains revision-driven and uses `requestAnimationFrame`, hidden-tab suspension and bounded views.

## Runtime and performance

The engine uses `WeakMap` runtime indexes for workforce, household, housing, municipal, emergency, nutrition, treasury, political-profile and realm-profile lookup. Runtime caches are not serialized.

Long-run state remains bounded:

- detailed annual state is compacted after the retention window;
- incident history is capped;
- political blocs are aggregate objects instead of citizen entities;
- no protester, voter, policeman or individual refugee entities are created by v2.8.

## Determinism and idempotency

The v2.8 regression gate checks:

- complete settlement and realm coverage;
- bounded political values;
- six political blocs;
- Universal Encyclopedia integration;
- runtime-index reuse;
- same-year idempotency for treasury and stability effects;
- deterministic `4 years == 2 + 2 years` simulation;
- v2.7 nutrition determinism under political feedback;
- save/export round-trip fingerprints;
- an extreme food/fiscal/labor crisis producing real civil disorder and a state response.

## Runtime integration

`nutrition-loader.js` now chains `politics-loader.js`, preserving the existing late-loader architecture. The simulation bridge is installed after v2.7 so yearly order becomes:

`core simulation → workforce → households → housing → municipal → emergency → nutrition → politics`

This keeps v2.8 additive and avoids rewriting stable older systems.
