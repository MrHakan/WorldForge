# WorldForge v2.5 — Municipal Infrastructure & Public Services

v2.5 turns the physical urban growth introduced in v2.4 into a serviced civic system. Dense housing, population growth and district inequality now create infrastructure demand that must be financed, maintained and staffed.

## Nine public-service systems

Every settlement tracks explicit coverage, demand and persistent infrastructure stock for:

1. Water Supply
2. Sanitation & Sewerage
3. Roads & Streets
4. Waste Collection
5. Fire Protection
6. Schools & Education
7. Clinics & Public Health
8. Public Transport
9. Street Lighting

Coverage is not a decorative score. Each service has demand, infrastructure stock, annual depreciation and investment. A settlement can therefore inherit good infrastructure, underinvest for decades and accumulate a maintenance backlog even if its population remains prosperous.

## Demand from the existing simulation

Service demand is derived from existing WorldForge state rather than generated independently:

- population and age structure;
- v2.4 housing density;
- overcrowding and slum pressure;
- settlement urban class;
- household count;
- migration-driven urban expansion.

Rapid urbanization therefore increases the load on water, sewerage, roads, waste collection, transit and emergency coverage.

## Capacity and institutions

Service capacity uses already simulated resources:

- Public Service and Administration workers;
- Construction and Engineering workers;
- Health and Education workers;
- Transport and Logistics workers;
- Hospitals;
- Universities and libraries;
- settlement technology adoption.

Hospitals and universities now have direct local service consequences instead of existing only as broader institutional modifiers.

## Municipal finance

Each settlement receives a bounded annual civic budget composed of:

- property-tax revenue derived from the v2.4 property tax base;
- local fees linked to population and prosperity;
- limited realm grants linked to the sovereign treasury;
- retained municipal reserves.

Realm grants are deducted from the actual treasury account. The same simulation year is idempotent, so repeated UI/bootstrap calls cannot charge the realm twice or apply the same municipal feedback twice.

Spending is distributed between services according to weighted local demand. Infrastructure that receives too little investment depreciates and creates maintenance backlog.

## District service inequality

Existing v2.4 district summaries are extended rather than duplicated. Districts receive bounded service summaries for:

- service access;
- health risk;
- fire risk;
- inequality gap.

Wealthier and central districts generally obtain stronger effective access, while Slums, Low Quarter and Outskirts are more vulnerable to crowding and service shortfalls. This produces visible intra-city inequality without creating an unbounded number of persistent households, buildings or utility-network entities.

## Causal feedback

```text
Population + housing density
 → infrastructure demand
 → municipal revenue / realm grants
 → service investment
 → infrastructure stock
 → coverage / maintenance backlog
 → health, education and safety
 → property values / migration / slum pressure
 → future population and tax base
```

Important feedbacks include:

- weak water and sanitation increase public-health risk;
- health-service coverage affects demographic outcomes;
- school coverage feeds education and literacy;
- roads and transit contribute to urban service quality;
- lighting and waste coverage affect city pressure;
- high-quality public services support property values and development;
- weak service coverage increases outward migration pressure and slum persistence.

## Civic Services Board

The new workspace exposes:

- world mean service coverage;
- municipal revenue and spending;
- realm grants;
- maintenance backlog;
- health and fire risk;
- district inequality;
- settlement-level service coverage;
- weakest local services;
- municipal budgets and reserves;
- standalone Civic Ledger JSON export.

Municipal Services, Public Infrastructure and Service Inequality are also first-class Universal Encyclopedia article types.

## Optimization and save-state discipline

v2.5 continues the optimization work from v2.4.

### Indexed runtime access

Municipal simulation uses WeakMap-backed indexes for:

- housing profiles;
- household profiles;
- workforce markets;
- cities;
- realm treasury accounts;
- active institution counts;
- municipal profiles.

These runtime caches are not serialized into world saves.

### Bounded persistent state

WorldForge stores one municipal profile per settlement plus compact service summaries for existing districts. It does not persist every pipe, street lamp, sewer segment, garbage vehicle or individual dwelling.

### Rendering

The Civic Services Board is revision-driven, coalesces DOM work through `requestAnimationFrame`, polls at a low cadence and suspends periodic rendering while the browser tab is hidden.

### Regression coverage

The v2.5 CI gate first re-runs the v2.4 housing regression, then verifies:

- all nine service systems;
- municipal profile coverage;
- service values remain bounded;
- budget and backlog accounting;
- district service inequality;
- Universal Encyclopedia integration;
- runtime cache reuse;
- same-year simulation idempotency;
- realm grants cannot be charged twice;
- deterministic `4 years == 2 + 2 years` simulation;
- housing, household and workforce determinism after municipal feedback;
- save/export round-trip stability.

## Natural next step

A future **v2.6 — Health, Disease, Fire & Emergency Response** can consume the risk values created by v2.5. Water and sanitation failures, crowding, hospital capacity and trade connectivity can drive outbreaks, while density, building condition, water access and fire coverage can drive urban fires. Emergency services, quarantines, disasters and recovery spending would then turn infrastructure quality into historical events with demographic and fiscal consequences.
