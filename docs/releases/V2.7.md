# WorldForge v2.7 — Food, Nutrition, Famine & Humanitarian Relief

v2.7 connects the physical World Market, household poverty, war logistics, municipal services and emergency response into one food-security system. Food crises are no longer represented only by a generic famine event: settlements now maintain aggregate nutrition state, rationing status, malnutrition, famine stages, humanitarian demand, relief deliveries and displacement.

## Aggregate nutrition model

WorldForge continues to avoid person-by-person simulation. Each settlement stores a bounded food and nutrition profile:

- nutrition index;
- food access;
- affordability;
- diet diversity;
- malnutrition rate;
- severe malnutrition rate;
- famine stage;
- rationing status;
- humanitarian relief need and delivery;
- nutrition-related excess mortality;
- displaced population and refugee-camp population.

The model consumes existing simulation data rather than introducing an isolated random crisis generator.

## Food basket

The v1.8 World Market already contains physical food commodities:

- Grain;
- Rice;
- Fish;
- Livestock;
- Wine as a minor dietary component.

v2.7 converts those stocks into a normalized food-equivalent basket using each commodity's food value. Food access also reads:

- market `foodSecurity`;
- food days;
- food shortages;
- food prices relative to base prices;
- household poverty;
- household disposable income;
- diet diversity.

This means a settlement can have food somewhere in the world and still experience hunger if its households cannot afford the local basket or war prevents delivery.

## Food-security stages

The bounded nutrition index maps to six stages:

1. Secure;
2. Stressed;
3. Crisis;
4. Emergency;
5. Famine;
6. Catastrophe.

The stage is not a cosmetic label. It controls rationing, humanitarian demand, mortality pressure, displacement and whether the crisis enters canonical History.

## Siege and blockade pressure

Active military operations now have a direct civilian food consequence.

An active siege increases food-access pressure and strongly reduces humanitarian route effectiveness. An active naval blockade applies a separate logistics penalty. Combined siege and blockade conditions can reduce convoy delivery to a small fraction of loaded cargo.

```text
Physical food stock
 + market prices
 + household poverty
 + siege / blockade
 + disease pressure
 + public-health capacity
 → food access
 → nutrition / malnutrition
 → famine stage
 → mortality / displacement / migration
```

## Humanitarian relief convoys

Relief is physical.

When a settlement reaches Crisis or worse, v2.7 searches indexed World Market state for a donor settlement with a real food surplus. Preference is given to donors in the same realm, while cross-realm donors remain possible when no local reserve is adequate.

A convoy:

1. removes food-equivalent cargo from the donor's real commodity inventory;
2. applies route losses from siege and blockade pressure;
3. adds only delivered cargo to the recipient's real market inventory;
4. records loaded, delivered and lost quantities;
5. records route effectiveness;
6. charges humanitarian response costs.

No food is created from nothing by the relief system.

## Humanitarian finance

Relief response first uses a bounded share of available municipal reserves. Remaining funded cost can draw from the owning realm's treasury, subject to a strict treasury percentage cap.

Same-year simulation is idempotent, preventing duplicated:

- convoy loading;
- treasury deductions;
- municipal relief spending;
- nutrition deaths;
- History events.

## Malnutrition and excess mortality

Food insecurity creates aggregate malnutrition and severe-malnutrition rates. Emergency, Famine and Catastrophe stages can produce bounded excess mortality.

The model also reads current disease severity and disease hazard from v2.6. Existing epidemics therefore make food crises more dangerous, while weak healthcare, water and sanitation reduce the settlement's health buffer.

This produces combined crises without simulating individual patients or individual meals.

## Rationing

Crisis-stage settlements and settlements under siege or blockade activate explicit rationing state. Rationing is exposed to UI, exports and the Universal Encyclopedia.

Future releases can use this state for political legitimacy, black markets or unrest without changing the v2.7 save schema.

## Displacement and refugee camps

Severe food crises create aggregate displaced population. A share persists in a bounded refugee-camp population that decays naturally when conditions improve.

Refugee camps feed:

- migration pressure;
- emergency nutrition risk metadata;
- municipal humanitarian pressure;
- Encyclopedia articles;
- Humanitarian Board metrics.

The system does not create thousands of individual refugee entities.

## History integration

Famine-stage crises can become canonical `famine` History events.

Before creating a new event, v2.7 checks whether the older World Market system already created a famine event for the same settlement and year. This avoids duplicate famine history while allowing the richer nutrition state to exist alongside v1.8.

## Universal Encyclopedia

v2.7 adds four article types:

- `Nutrition Status`;
- `Famine Crisis`;
- `Relief Operation`;
- `Refugee Camp`.

Articles cross-link settlements with household, municipal and emergency state. Relief-operation articles preserve donor, recipient, loaded cargo, delivered cargo, losses and route effectiveness.

## Optimization

v2.7 continues the bounded-state architecture used since v2.3.

### Runtime indexes

WeakMap-backed runtime indexes provide direct access to:

- World Market settlement markets;
- household profiles;
- municipal profiles;
- emergency profiles;
- workforce markets;
- treasury realms;
- nutrition profiles;
- active siege settlement IDs;
- active blockade settlement IDs.

These indexes are runtime-only and are never serialized.

### Bounded persistent state

The simulation stores one nutrition profile per settlement plus bounded incident and convoy ledgers. It does not store individual meals, aid workers, refugees, convoy vehicles or household food items.

### Rendering

The Humanitarian & Food Security Board follows the same optimized UI model as v2.4–v2.6:

- revision-driven rendering;
- `requestAnimationFrame` coalescing;
- hidden-tab suspension;
- limited visible cards and ledgers.

## Determinism and regression coverage

The v2.7 gate re-runs the full v2.6 emergency regression before the new nutrition regression. It verifies:

- one nutrition profile per settlement;
- bounded nutrition and malnutrition metrics;
- valid famine-stage range;
- Universal Encyclopedia integration;
- WeakMap runtime-cache reuse;
- same-year death idempotency;
- same-year treasury idempotency;
- same-year physical food-transfer idempotency;
- deterministic `4 years == 2 + 2 years` caller chunking;
- preservation of v2.6 emergency determinism;
- preservation of municipal, housing, household and workforce determinism;
- save/export round-trip stability;
- physical staple exhaustion plus siege/blockade can create famine;
- severe famine creates bounded excess mortality;
- famine enters canonical History without duplicating existing resource famine events;
- a stocked donor physically loses cargo when a real relief convoy is delivered;
- relief transfers cannot create negative food inventory.

## Causal chain

```text
Climate / agriculture / trade / war
 → physical food stocks and prices
 → household affordability
 → food access and nutrition
 → malnutrition / famine
 → mortality / displacement / migration
 → humanitarian relief demand
 → donor stocks + municipal / treasury spending
 → delivered aid
 → future population, labor, market and public-service conditions
```

## Natural next step

A natural **v2.8 — Politics, Unrest, Legitimacy & Civil Disorder** release can consume the social consequences now produced by the simulation: food rationing, high prices, unemployment, inequality, conscription, famine, disease, housing pressure, taxation and emergency failures. It can model legitimacy, protests, strikes, riots, repression, reform, coups and revolutions as aggregate political processes without individual-citizen simulation.
