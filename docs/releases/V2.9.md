# WorldForge v2.9 — Diplomacy, Treaties, Trade Agreements & International Relations

v2.9 extends the living-world simulation beyond domestic politics. Every active realm now maintains persistent bilateral relations with every other active realm, and those relationships are shaped by war, trade, military imbalance, domestic instability, famine, fiscal capacity and treaty history.

## Bilateral relations

Each canonical realm pair tracks trust, goodwill, fear, rivalry, trade dependence, border tension, claim pressure, diplomatic tension, war risk, alliance affinity and trade access. These values persist across years instead of being regenerated as isolated random events.

## Treaty system

The treaty ledger supports eight persistent agreement types:

- Trade Agreement
- Non-Aggression Pact
- Defensive Pact
- Alliance
- Embargo
- Sanctions
- Peace Treaty
- Humanitarian Aid Accord

Treaties have signing years, expiry years, strength, status and directional fields where required for embargoes, sanctions and aid.

## Material diplomatic consequences

Diplomacy is connected to the existing economy rather than existing only as flavour text. Trade agreements can transfer bounded food surpluses toward a partner experiencing shortages. Humanitarian aid spends the donor realm's real treasury and moves real food reserves into recipient markets. Embargoes and sanctions reduce trade access, raise target market pressure and prices, and feed domestic political grievance.

The resulting causal loops include:

`trade pact → food access ↑ → market pressure ↓ → domestic grievance ↓ → legitimacy ↑`

`embargo → trade access ↓ → prices ↑ → merchant/commoner grievance ↑ → political pressure ↑`

`famine abroad → humanitarian aid → donor treasury ↓ + recipient food reserves ↑ → goodwill/trust ↑`

`rivalry + border tension + territorial claims + fear → diplomatic tension ↑ → war risk ↑ → diplomatic crisis`

## Domestic politics feedback

Foreign policy now feeds back into v2.8 Politics. Successful cooperation and aid can improve legitimacy, while embargoes, sanctions and foreign pressure can increase grievance and merchant opposition. Domestic revolutionary/secession pressure also influences international claim pressure and peace-conference willingness.

## Diplomatic Chancery

The new UI workspace provides:

- global treaty, alliance, embargo, aid and war-risk KPIs;
- bilateral relation cards with trust, tension, rivalry, war risk, trade access and alliance affinity;
- realm foreign-policy profiles with reputation, treaty reliability, isolation and foreign pressure;
- active treaty ledger;
- diplomatic incident stream;
- JSON Diplomatic Ledger export.

## Universal Encyclopedia

New article types:

- `diplomatic_relation`
- `realm_diplomacy`
- `treaty`
- `diplomatic_incident`

These cross-link into existing realm politics and History chronology.

## Determinism and performance

v2.9 uses one aggregate object per realm pair rather than simulating individual diplomats, merchants or delegations. Runtime lookups use `WeakMap` indexes for treasuries, politics, markets, nutrition, military power, bilateral trade and relation profiles. Incidents and detailed annual ledgers are bounded and compacted for long simulations.

The regression gate verifies pair coverage, bounded relation metrics, Encyclopedia integration, indexed-read reuse, same-year idempotency, deterministic caller chunking, save/export round trips, embargo market effects, humanitarian treasury/food transfers and high-hostility war-risk escalation.
