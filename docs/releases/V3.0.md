# WorldForge v3.0 — Geopolitical Blocs, Great Powers & World Orders

WorldForge v3.0 promotes the v2.9 bilateral diplomacy layer into a systemic international order. Realms are ranked by composite capability, powerful states acquire prestige and influence, treaty networks become geopolitical blocs, weaker states enter spheres of influence, and rival blocs can create arms races, contested spheres and international congresses.

## Power hierarchy

Each active realm receives a persistent power profile with:

- rank and tier (`great_power`, `major_power`, `regional_power`, `minor_power`);
- composite power score and world power share;
- prestige, influence and strategic reach;
- military, fiscal, economic, population and diplomatic weights;
- balancing pressure and hegemonic challenge;
- primary geopolitical bloc and sphere-client count.

Power is not a decorative score. It is derived from WorldForge's existing military forces, treasury/revenue/debt position, physical market economy, population/workforce footprint, diplomatic reputation, treaty reliability and domestic legitimacy.

## Geopolitical blocs

Active alliances and defensive pacts are converted into connected security blocs. Strong trade networks can also form trade leagues. Blocs track:

- leader realm;
- member list;
- cohesion;
- combined world power share;
- bloc prestige;
- founding and dissolution years.

Blocs are recomputed from the live treaty network while retaining persistent identities where membership remains unchanged. Members of the same security bloc receive small bounded trust/stability feedback; competing security blocs create balancing and rivalry pressure instead of an isolated pair-only diplomatic model.

## Spheres of influence

Great and major powers can become patrons of weaker realms when capability asymmetry is combined with trust, goodwill, trade dependence and low hostility. Each sphere relation tracks patron, client, influence intensity and possible competing challenger.

Contested spheres feed rivalry and diplomatic tension between competing patrons and create persistent world-order crisis records. This establishes a foundation for future protectorates, client states and proxy conflicts without creating unbounded per-agent state.

## World-order model

The international system is classified as one of:

- `unipolar`;
- `bipolar`;
- `multipolar`;
- `bloc_competition`;
- `fragmented`.

The order also tracks:

- hegemon / leading power;
- power concentration;
- great-power gap;
- stability;
- arms-race index;
- bloc tension;
- proxy-risk pressure.

World-order shifts are written into History when polarity changes after the simulation is established.

## International congresses

When great-power competition, bloc tension or contested spheres drive stability low enough, a deterministic international congress may be convened. Congresses include leading-power participants and apply bounded de-escalation to the most important power relationship. Some congresses also create a short non-aggression regime through the existing v2.9 treaty engine.

Congress records remain persistent and are exposed through the Universal Encyclopedia.

## Product surfaces

### World Order Observatory

The new workspace shows:

- order type and hegemon;
- great-power count and active blocs;
- world-order stability and arms-race pressure;
- complete power ranking;
- geopolitical bloc membership/cohesion;
- spheres of influence and contested clients;
- international congress history.

The workspace exports a **World Order Ledger** JSON containing power profiles, blocs, spheres, congresses, crises, annual order data and runtime-performance metrics.

### Universal Encyclopedia

v3.0 adds:

- `great_power`;
- `geopolitical_bloc`;
- `sphere_of_influence`;
- `world_order`;
- `international_congress`.

## Determinism and optimization

v3.0 keeps aggregate realm-level state rather than individual diplomatic-agent entities. Runtime indexes are stored in `WeakMap` caches so they do not increase save size. UI rendering uses revision keys, `requestAnimationFrame`, hidden-tab suspension and a slower polling interval.

The dedicated regression gate validates:

- v2.9 Diplomacy compatibility;
- complete realm power-profile coverage;
- normalized world power shares;
- runtime-index reuse;
- same-year idempotency;
- deterministic `4 years == 2 + 2` simulation;
- save/export round trips;
- security-bloc formation from alliance networks;
- sphere-of-influence formation from asymmetric friendly relations;
- international-congress de-escalation under severe rival-bloc tension.

## Causal chain

```text
Military + Treasury + Markets + Population + Diplomacy + Legitimacy
  -> Power hierarchy / prestige / influence
  -> Alliances and trade networks
  -> Security blocs / trade leagues
  -> Spheres of influence
  -> Balancing pressure / contested spheres
  -> Arms race / bloc tension / proxy risk
  -> World-order stability and polarity
  -> International congresses / de-escalation
  -> feedback into bilateral diplomacy
```
