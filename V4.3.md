# WorldForge v4.3 — Heritage, Museums, Relics & Archaeological Expeditions

WorldForge v4.3 extends the Living Ruins & Archaeology layer into a cultural-heritage system. Archaeological sites can now enter heritage registers, museums curate collections, relics retain deterministic provenance and custody, expeditions operate at field sites, restitution claims can emerge across realms, and looting, smuggling, wartime removal and rescue incidents become visible parts of world history.

## Heritage registry

Every v4.2 archaeological site receives a deterministic heritage score derived from age, landmark importance, preservation, finds, settlement status and current archaeological condition. The score maps to five protection levels:

1. **Unlisted**
2. **Local Register**
3. **Protected**
4. **National Heritage**
5. **World Heritage**

Registered sites track modeled conservation risk, protection budgets, custodianship, public access and emergency-planning status. World-heritage designation is an in-world WorldForge category rather than a direct simulation of a real-world organization.

## Museums

Settlements with significant archaeology can support local, regional or national museums. Capitals receive priority for national institutions. Each museum tracks:

- collection capacity;
- security;
- conservation capability;
- prestige;
- displayed and stored relic counts;
- disputed collection objects;
- linked heritage sites.

Museum placement is deterministic and appears directly in the Pixel Civilization settlement renderer.

## Relics and provenance

Artifacts and inscriptions from v4.2 become first-class relic records. Each relic stores:

- cultural and political origin;
- material/category;
- estimated date;
- significance and condition;
- current holder and holder realm;
- display state;
- conservation priority;
- possible illicit-transfer flags;
- a deterministic provenance chain.

Custody can remain at the site or move to museums, state collections or private collections. Some deterministic transfers cross realm boundaries and can become disputed.

## Archaeological expeditions

Significant sites can receive field projects with:

- planned, active, completed or suspended status;
- permits;
- sponsoring museums;
- team size and specialization;
- budgets;
- risk;
- progress;
- linked discoveries.

The system remains bounded and derived from existing archaeology rather than creating an unbounded actor simulation.

## Restitution and heritage security

Disputed foreign-held or suspiciously transferred relics can generate restitution claims. Claims record claimant and holder realms, legal/cultural basis, public support, diplomatic tension and status.

The heritage-security layer can also derive:

- looting attempts;
- smuggling cases;
- wartime removals;
- emergency rescue operations.

These records connect relic custody, conflict pressure, conservation risk and archaeological context without replacing the diplomacy or warfare engines.

## Pixel Civilization

v4.3 adds a visual heritage layer on top of the v4.2 renderer:

- registered sites receive protection-seal markers;
- museums receive dedicated pixel structures;
- relics can be rendered as deterministic collection cards with holder, dispute, significance, condition and provenance metadata.

The existing v4.1.1 architectural variation and v4.2 living-ruin visuals remain intact.

## Worldbuilding Atelier

The Worldbuilding Atelier gains a new **Heritage, Museums & Relics** section containing:

- global heritage KPIs;
- local heritage registry cards;
- museum network cards;
- relic focus and provenance timeline;
- archaeological expedition records;
- restitution disputes;
- heritage-security incidents;
- a deterministic heritage ledger JSON export.

## Universal Encyclopedia

The Encyclopedia gains six new record types:

- `heritage_site`
- `museum`
- `relic`
- `archaeological_expedition`
- `restitution_claim`
- `heritage_incident`

These records cross-link to v4.2 archaeological sites, museums, relics, field projects, claims and incidents.

## State and compatibility

The new layer is additive under `world.heritage` and uses its own schema/version marker. v4.3 does not replace `world.archaeology` or `world.worldbuilding`.

The dedicated v4.3 regression suite validates:

- heritage scoring and protection states;
- museum collection totals;
- provenance chains;
- deterministic Pixel Civilization overlays;
- relic cards;
- Universal Encyclopedia indexing;
- foreign-holder restitution claims;
- save/export round trips;
- heritage fingerprints;
- preservation of v4.2 archaeology and v4.x worldbuilding fingerprints.

The v4.3 CI gate also reruns the v4.0, v4.1, v4.1.1 and v4.2 regression suites before the new heritage test.
